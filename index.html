What this is

Each axis runs between two named positions, and each tradition is placed somewhere along it. A higher number means further toward the right-hand pole — not more, and not better. The colour darkens along the same range, so a row read left to right shows the shape of a tradition’s position rather than any verdict about it.

Nothing here measures faithfulness, truth, or authenticity. Distance from a pole is distance, not error.

Not every belief fits an axis. Some questions divide traditions over whether the question even applies; some almost no tradition has ruled on; one or two are taught by a single tradition alone. Those are asked in the third survey and reported without a score, alongside what a range of traditions actually teach. Where something cannot be placed on the spectrum, that is said rather than forced.

This is a draft. Corrections are wanted.

It is unpublished and unreviewed, built in the first instance for my own use. Some placements are wrong; I would rather know which. The most useful correction names four things:

  1. The tradition — which of the 30
  2. The axis — which of the 17
  3. The score you would assign instead — a number from 0 to 100
  4. A source — a confession, catechism, official statement or standard reference
