What is wrong with this chart

Stated plainly, because a reader is better served by knowing the weaknesses than by discovering them. Each is followed by what version 7 does about it. None is fully cured.

  • The three spectrums are partly imposed. Correlating the axes across the traditions produces a clean split into two groups, not three. The three-way arrangement used here is a conceptual overlay on that result, chosen because it is easier to read and because each part has a headline a non-specialist can hold. It is a presentational decision, not a finding. Version 7: both groupings are now on the page. The View menu offers the conceptual three and, separately, the two clusters the data actually produce, computed live from the scores so that any correction to a score updates them. “Axis correlations” shows the full table they are built from.
  • Sabbath observance does not belong anywhere. It is the most independent axis on the chart, correlating with the other core axes at an average of only 0.19 (absolute value, computed from the current scores). Version 7: it no longer sits in Worship and Practice. It stands on its own, marked S. The computed clusters still have to put it somewhere, and they place it only loosely; with all seventeen axes counted and the data split three ways, it separates out alone.
  • The scores are judgments. They are better read as ordinal than cardinal: that one tradition sits further along an axis than another is the claim, not the digit. Reasonable people will disagree with individual placements, and some of those disagreements will be right. Version 7: “Read as: Order only” replaces every digit with a rank, ties shared, and the totals with a mean rank. The judgments remain judgments.
  • The axis set is not neutral. Choosing these fourteen rather than nine or twenty shapes every result, including every total. Several obvious axes are missing, and their absence is itself a distortion. Version 7: the totals are no longer fixed. “Totals count” lets you choose which axes are added, and the order changes as you do. Two missing axes are drafted: women in ministry and church and state. They are marked provisional and left out of the totals unless you add them. The model of the atonement is shown as categories, not scores, because the models are alternatives, not points on a line. Intercession of Mary and the saints is held back on purpose for the coming multifaith edition, where it fits a wider axis on intermediaries between the worshipper and God.
  • Several labels cover internally diverse families. Quakers span programmed evangelical to liberal unprogrammed; Lutherans span the ELCA to the WELS. A single row for a family that split over a doctrine cannot represent it honestly. Version 7: Quakers now have two rows, programmed and unprogrammed. Lutherans have three: the ELCA, the LCMS and the WELS. Other broad labels remain, including Presbyterian / Reformed, which covers both the PC(USA) and the PCA.
  • Some axes conflate two things. Baptism mixed mode with necessity, so a tradition practising symbolic immersion could outrank one practising regenerative infant baptism. That was a defect in the axis, not a claim about the traditions. Version 7: the axis is split. Baptism: Efficacy runs from symbol to necessity, and has been rescored. Baptism: Candidates runs from infants to believers only, and is provisional. Mode is a matter of categories, not degree, so it now appears in the survey’s third set instead. Church and state still joins two ideas, institutional separation and withdrawal from politics, and its placement notes say so.
  • The author is not neutral either. This was built by a Latter-day Saint attorney. The chart tries to describe rather than to argue. No tradition is highlighted by default — use the highlight selector above to pick out your own, or anyone else’s, and read the rest against it.

If any of that describes a problem you can fix — a placement, an axis, a label, a missing dimension — please write. A correction naming the tradition, the axis, the score you would assign, and a source is worth more than a general objection, but a general objection is welcome too. Scores on the three provisional axes are especially open to correction, and suggestions for axes that ought to exist are especially wanted.

On the scores

Positions reflect official doctrine and scholarly consensus, not judgments of value, truth or authenticity. They are better read as ordinal than cardinal: the ordering is the claim, not the digit. Reasonable disagreement is expected. Where an axis does not apply — candidates for baptism in a tradition that has no water baptism — the cell reads n/a and is left out of totals rather than scored.

On the totals

A total is the sum of the axis scores you have chosen to count, shown against the most that tradition could score on them. By default it counts the fourteen core axes. Because the choice of axes determines every total, the totals are illustrative and the individual axes are the real content. On the core axes a total measures distance from the historic Nicene-and-Reformation cluster — distance, not error.

On the labels

Several labels still cover internally diverse families. Scores target the representative center unless a more specific label is listed. Inspired by Dr. Matt Baker’s Christian Denominations Family Tree.

This is a draft. Corrections are wanted.

Some of these placements are wrong. I would rather know which. The most useful correction names four things — anything at that level of specificity can be acted on directly, where a general objection cannot:

  1. The tradition — which of the 30
  2. The axis — which of the 17
  3. The score you would assign instead — a number between 0 and 100
  4. A source — a confession, catechism, official statement or standard reference work
