Honor, Patronage, and Sacred Space

A Social-Scientific Reading of the Temple Action of Jesus (Matt 21:12–17)

By S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

The question

The usual reading of the temple action is a reading of temperament: Jesus loses his patience with the traders and drives them out in anger. Matthew’s scene does not stop at the tables, though. It goes on, without a break, to the blind and the lame being healed in the temple and to children crying “Hosanna to the Son of David” loudly enough that the chief priests object (21:14–16).

This paper asks what the whole scene looks like when it is read with the tools social scientists use for the ancient Mediterranean world, rather than with a modern assumption about anger.

Three lenses

Honor. Public acts in a temple court were claims made before witnesses, and claims invite challenge. Matthew records the challenge the next day: “By what authority doest thou these things?” (21:23).

Patronage. The temple stood between worshippers and God, and its personnel controlled access. In Matthew’s scene the blind and lame come to Jesus in the temple and are healed there, with no intermediary.

Sacred space. The saying he quotes is about what the space is for: “My house shall be called the house of prayer” (Isaiah 56:7). The question becomes who decides the use of the house, and on whose authority.

Why it matters

Read together, the lenses describe a scene of authority and restored use, not of disorder. The paper treats verses 14–17 as part of the event rather than its aftermath, and argues that they change what the overturned tables mean.
