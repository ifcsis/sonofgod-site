One Cleansing or Two

Why the Scholarship Shifted

By S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

The question

John tells the clearing of the temple at the first Passover of the ministry; Matthew, Mark and Luke tell it in the last week. The core acts match, and the details and sayings differ. Every answer, one cleansing or two, is an answer to that table.

Why 1905 counted two

The books an English-speaking scholar reached for in 1905 — Farrar, Geikie, Edersheim, Andrews, the Broadus harmony — all counted two. They read the four Gospels as four chronicles to be fitted into one timeline, so a different placement meant a different event. The method goes back to Augustine’s Harmony of the Gospels, about AD 400. James E. Talmage’s Jesus the Christ followed those sources.

The other reading was already present in his day, mostly in German and critical work: Neander judged a repeat “hardly probable,” and a 1902 survey in Bibliotheca Sacra set out all three positions as live.

What changed

No single discovery moved the question. Scholars stopped reading the Gospels as chronicles and began reading them as arranged testimony. Once an evangelist could place an event for its meaning, one cleansing told twice became the simpler reading.

Where it stands

By 2015 Allan Chapple, who himself defends two cleansings, called one late cleansing the view of “the overwhelming majority.” It is a majority, not a settlement: a standing group of commentators still holds two, and a minority of one-event scholars places the single event early, where John has it. “Generally” is the honest word.
